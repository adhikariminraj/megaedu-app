## 1. Understanding MEGA.EDU

MEGA.EDU connects everyone involved in a student's education — the school, its teachers, the student, and the student's parents — around one continuous flow of information:

```
School  →  Teachers  →  Students  →  Academic Activities  →  Assessment  →  Progress  →  Results
```

In plain terms, this means:

- A **school** sets itself up on MEGA.EDU — its sessions (school years), its grades and sections, and the subjects it teaches.
- **Teachers** are assigned to grades, sections, and subjects, so the system always knows who is responsible for what.
- **Students** are placed into a grade (and, if the school uses them, a section) for the current school year.
- Day to day, teachers record **academic activities** for their students — attendance, chapter-by-chapter teaching progress, quick unit tests, and written evaluations.
- Periodically, teachers and admins record **assessment** results against the school's own marking scheme — the marks or grades that will eventually form a Report Card.
- All of this together builds a student's **progress** picture — visible, in an appropriate form, to the student and their parents.
- At the end of the process, that progress becomes **results** — published subject grades, a GPA, and a full Report Card — and, once a term or year ends, a **promotion decision** about what happens next.

Every part of this guide walks through one piece of that flow, in the order a school would actually use it: set the school up, manage the class day to day, run assessments, publish results, and finally record promotion decisions.

**One rule worth knowing from the start**: MEGA.EDU never shows a Parent or Student an assessment result until a teacher or admin has explicitly **published** it. A mark being entered is not the same as a mark being visible — see [Section 6](#6-assessment--results) for exactly how this works.

## 2. Getting Started

### What is MEGA.EDU?

MEGA.EDU is a platform that gives every school a digital identity and gives every teacher, student, and parent one account — a **MEGA ID** — that works everywhere on the platform. A student's records follow them, even if their school affiliation changes later.

### Who uses it?

- **School Administrators** — set up and run the school's presence on MEGA.EDU: sessions, grades, sections, subjects, staff, students, attendance, evaluations, assessments, and promotion.
- **Teachers** — manage the classes and subjects they're assigned to: attendance, teaching progress, unit tests, evaluations, and (if they teach a subject) assessment marks.
- **Students** — see their own progress: attendance, teaching progress, test results, shared evaluations, and — once published — assessment results and their Report Card.
- **Parents** — see the same progress information for each of their linked children, plus any scheduled Parent-Teacher Meetings.
- **Organization Admins** — publish free online courses through MEGA Academy (a separate part of the platform from a school's own academic records).

### User roles, in one line each

| Role | In one line |
|---|---|
| School Administrator | Runs the school's account — everything from creating the school year to publishing results |
| Teacher | Teaches specific grades/sections/subjects and records progress and marks for them |
| Student | Views their own school record, progress, and results |
| Parent | Views the same information for each of their children |
| Organization Admin | Publishes free courses on MEGA Academy |

A single person can hold more than one role at once (for example, a Teacher who is also a Parent) — MEGA.EDU shows whichever dashboard is most relevant when they sign in.

## 3. School Administration

### Managing school information

**What is this?** Your school's public profile on MEGA.EDU — name, contact details, location, and the description visitors see.

**Who can use it?** Any School Administrator for that school.

**How to use it:** From your dashboard's Profile tab, edit your school's description, contact email/phone, location, and the grades your school offers, and post Programs, News, and Opportunities for your school community.

**Important rules:** Your school only appears in the public school directory once a Platform Administrator has verified it — verification is a one-time review done by MEGA.EDU, not something you can self-approve.

### Your school's logo

**What is this?** Your school's own logo, shown on your School Directory card, your public school profile, and your dashboard, instead of a generic placeholder.

**Who can use it?** Any School Administrator for that school.

**How to use it:** From your dashboard's Profile tab, find the **School Logo** section. Choose **Upload logo** and pick a PNG, JPEG, or WebP image up to 2MB.

**Changing your logo:** Uploading a new image replaces the current one.

**Removing your logo:** Choose **Remove** to clear it.

**No logo yet?** Your school still looks intentional, not broken — MEGA.EDU shows a clean, bordered monogram made from your school's initials (for example, "SA" for Sunrise Academy) everywhere the logo would otherwise appear.

### Academic sessions

**What is this?** An Academic Session is one school year on MEGA.EDU. Everything else — grades, teacher assignments, student placements — happens *inside* a session.

**Who can use it?** School Administrator only.

**How to use it:** The first time you set up your school, you'll create your first session as part of the guided setup. When a school year ends, you use **New Session** (found from the Grades area of your dashboard) to close the current session and open the next one.

**Important rules:** A school can only have **one active session at a time**. When you start a new session, MEGA.EDU automatically carries forward any student whose promotion decision was already recorded — but a student with **no** decision recorded is never guessed at. They're placed in a clearly-visible "Pending" list instead, waiting for you to decide.

**Example:** Sunrise Academy finishes the 2025–2026 school year. The admin opens New Session, reviews a summary of what will happen to every student, and confirms. Every already-decided student (promoted or repeating) appears automatically in the new 2026–2027 session at the right grade. Two students whose decision was never recorded appear on the Pending list instead, waiting to be resolved — see [Section 8](#8-promotion).

### Grades and sections

**What is this?** A Grade is a class level your school offers (e.g. "Class 6"). A Section is an optional way to split a large grade into smaller groups (e.g. Class 6 → 6A, 6B, 6C).

**Who can use it?** School Administrator only.

**How to use it:** During setup, pick which grades your school uses and give each one a display name. Sections are optional — a school with small classes can simply not use them.

**Important rules:** Sections belong to the grade, not to a school year — the same section names carry across years. A section is never deleted, only deactivated, so a student's history always makes sense even years later. **A student's section is never automatically carried forward** into a new school year, and never automatically changes when they're promoted — the admin assigns it fresh each year, the same way teacher assignments work.

### Subjects

**What is this?** The list of subjects your school teaches (Mathematics, Science, English, etc.), and which subjects each grade offers in the current session.

**Who can use it?** School Administrator only.

**How to use it:** From the Academics page, add subjects to your school-wide catalog once, then choose which subjects each grade offers for the current session.

**Important rules:** A subject is defined once and reused every year, but which subjects a *grade* offers must be chosen fresh **every session** — nothing carries forward automatically, so a past year's curriculum always stays exactly as it was, even if the school changes its subjects later.

### Teachers and assignments

**What is this?** Assigning a teacher to a grade, and — more specifically — to teach a particular subject in a particular grade (and, if needed, just one section of it).

**Who can use it?** School Administrator only, from the Academics page.

**How to use it:** Assign a teacher to a subject either for the **whole grade** (every section) or for **just one section**. Multiple teachers can be assigned to the same subject at once — there's no "main" or "assistant" teacher concept.

**Important rules:** A teacher can't be assigned both "the whole grade" and "just one section" for the same subject at the same time — that would be a redundant, confusing double-assignment.

### Students

**What is this?** Adding students to your school and placing them into a grade (and section, if used).

**Who can use it?** School Administrator only.

**How to use it:** A student can join your school two ways: they self-register and you approve them, or you create their account directly (**+ Add Student**), which is useful for students who don't have their own device or email — you set a temporary password for them to use. You can place a student into a grade right when you create them, or afterward via **"Assign Grade & Section →"** on the Students tab.

**Important rules:** A student created directly by you is approved immediately (there's nothing to review, since you're the one creating the account). A student who self-registers stays pending until you approve them.

## 4. Class Overview

Opening any grade from your Grades page shows its **Class Overview** — everything about who's currently in that class, alongside the tools for recording promotion decisions.

### Viewing students by section

**What is this?** Every student currently in the grade, grouped by their section, with a final "Unassigned / No Section" group for anyone not yet placed into one.

**Who can use it?** School Administrator only.

**How to use it:** Open the grade from your Grades page. Students are listed section by section, each with a sequential "Roll No." within their own section (a display position for the list — not a permanently stored roll number).

### Newly enrolled students

**What is this?** A student who has just joined this grade for the first time this session — no history of repeating it.

**How it's shown:** They appear with a "Regular" status badge.

### Repeated students

**What is this?** A student whose *previous* year's decision was specifically "repeat this grade."

**How it's shown:** They appear with a "Repeated" status badge, based only on how they arrived in this grade — not on any decision that might already be recorded about what happens *next* year. A student who has already been decided to repeat *next* year still shows "Regular" today if their own arrival into this grade wasn't itself a repeat.

**Important rule:** A student stays visible in the Class Overview for the entire school year, even after a promotion decision has already been recorded for them — because they're still physically in the class until the year actually ends. This was a real fix made to the system: previously, a student who'd already been decided "repeating" could vanish from the class list mid-year, which wasn't accurate.

### Teacher and subject information

**What is this?** A list, above the student roster, of every teacher assigned to this grade and what subject(s) they teach — including whether a teacher only covers one specific section.

### Top 5 student ranking

**What is this?** The five highest-performing students in the **whole grade**, based on their published assessment results.

**How it works:** Ranking is calculated **once, across the entire grade** — before students are split into sections for display. This means a student in Section D can rank higher than a student in Section A, and that's expected: it's one class-wide ranking, not five separate section rankings.

**Important rule:** A student only appears in the ranking once they have at least one **published** assessment result. Draft (unpublished) marks are never used for ranking. Until any results anywhere in the grade have been published, the page simply says rankings will appear once results are published — it never shows an empty or misleading list.

### Understanding rankings

Ranking uses a student's GPA when at least one of their own published subjects resolves a numeric grade point, and falls back to their average result percentage otherwise — this is worked out per student, not as a single fixed setting for the whole school. It is always recalculated live — nothing is stored — so it's always current with the latest published results.

## 5. Academic Management

### Attendance

**What is this?** A daily record of whether each student was present, absent, late, or excused — one record per student, per day.

**Who can use it?** A School Administrator, or a teacher assigned as that grade/section's Class or Section Teacher.

**How to use it:** From the Attendance page, pick a date, pick the grade (and section, if applicable), and mark each student. If you need to fix a mistake, correcting an already-marked day keeps a record of exactly what changed and when — corrections are never silent.

**Important rules:** Attendance is taken once per student, per day — it's not tracked separately per subject.

### Student evaluations

**What is this?** A teacher's written comments about a student — either a general comment (from their Class/Section Teacher) or a subject-specific comment (from a Subject Teacher).

**Who can use it?** School Administrator, or the relevant teacher.

**How to use it:** From the Evaluations page (or the Subject Evaluations panel on a subject's own page), write a comment for a student, then choose whether to share it with the Parent, the Student, both, or keep it private for now.

**Important rules:** An evaluation can be freely edited while it's still private. **The moment it's shared with anyone**, every future edit to it is recorded — so a family can trust that what they were shown wasn't quietly changed afterward. Sharing is one-way: once shared, it stays shared. Sharing with a Parent and sharing with a Student are two completely separate choices — sharing with one never automatically shares with the other.

### Parent–Teacher Meetings

**What is this?** A scheduled meeting between a teacher and a student's parent(s) — either a single one-off meeting or several scheduled together (for example, a whole grade's PTM week).

**Who can use it?** School Administrator or an authorized teacher schedules meetings; parents can see their scheduled meetings but cannot request or create one themselves yet.

**How to use it:** Schedule a meeting with a date/time, location or online link, and (optionally) attach one of the student's evaluations for context. Afterward, mark it complete with outcome notes, or cancel it. A still-scheduled meeting can be rescheduled if needed.

**Important rule:** Parent-Teacher Meetings are visible to Parents and staff only — **students never see them**, on any surface, under any circumstance.

### Unit Tests

**What is this?** A quick, informal test tied to one specific teaching unit or chapter — separate from the school's formal assessment system (see [Section 6](#6-assessment--results)).

**Who can use it?** A teacher, for a unit they're teaching, once that unit has actually been started (not before).

**How to use it:** Create a test for the unit, then record each student's marks (or mark them absent) once it's been taken.

**Important rule:** Unit Tests and the formal Assessment system (below) are two genuinely separate things — a Unit Test score never automatically becomes part of a student's official assessment result, and the two are never merged.

## 6. Assessment & Results

This is the system that produces a student's official, report-card-ready results — separate from the quick Unit Tests above.

### The simplified workflow

```
Create Assessment System → Set Periods → Add Assessments → Choose How Results Are Shown
   → Apply to Grade/Subject → Enter Marks → Review Results → Publish → Report Card
```

Each step, explained:

1. **Create Assessment System** — give your marking scheme a name (e.g. "Class 4 Standard").
2. **Set Periods** — decide whether your school splits the year into terms (First Term, Second Term...) or just uses one overall assessment for the year.
3. **Add Assessments** — build the list of things students are assessed on (e.g. "Classwork," "Term Exam"), each with its own Full Marks.
4. **Choose How Results Are Shown** — decide whether results show as plain marks, or marks converted into a grade (like A+, A, B) using a grading scale.
5. **Apply to Grade/Subject** — decide whether this marking scheme applies to a whole grade, or is a special case for just one subject (e.g. Computer, which might be scored differently from every other subject).
6. **Enter Marks** — teachers record each student's actual result against each assessment.
7. **Review Results** — check everything is complete and correct before publishing.
8. **Publish** — make the results visible to students and parents.
9. **Report Card** — the finished, formatted view of everything, once published.

### Key terms explained

- **Marks** — the actual score a student earned on one assessment (e.g. 42 out of 50).
- **Full Marks** — the maximum possible score for that assessment (in the example above, 50). Internally this same number can represent either "raw marks" or a "percentage weight" — the school doesn't have to choose between two different systems; MEGA.EDU treats them the same way.
- **Terms/Periods** — an optional way to split assessments across the year (First Term, Second Term, Final/Annual). A school that doesn't use terms can skip this and use one flat list of assessments for the whole year.
- **Assessment components** — the individual, named things a student is scored on within one marking scheme (Classwork, Project, Term Exam, etc.).

### Marks-only results vs. marks with grades

A school can choose either:
- **Marks-only** — results show just the numbers (e.g. "42/50").
- **Marks with grades** — results also show a letter grade (like "A"), looked up from a grading scale the school defines (or a ready-made "standard grade levels" starting point, which the school can still customize).

Some assessments can also be **descriptive-only** — free-text comments instead of a number, for things that shouldn't be reduced to a mark.

### Publishing results

**What is this?** The single action that makes a subject's results visible to a Student and their Parent.

**Who can use it?** A School Administrator, or the Subject Teacher who teaches that subject.

**How to use it:** Once every required assessment has a mark entered for a student, publish their result for that subject.

**Important rule:** You **cannot publish an incomplete result** — if any required assessment is still missing a mark, that student is simply skipped when you publish (not treated as an error), and stays unpublished until every required piece is filled in. Descriptive-only assessments don't block publishing, since they're not numeric. Publishing is always for a **whole subject** at once — never part of it — so a family is never shown some marks while others are still hidden.

### Correcting results

**What is this?** Fixing a mistake in a result after it's already been published.

**Who can use it?** The same people who can publish — School Administrator or the Subject Teacher.

**How to use it:** Edit the result directly; the correction takes effect immediately.

**Important rule:** Correcting a published result **does not un-publish it** — the corrected value simply becomes the new visible value, and a record is kept of exactly what the value was before and after the correction, so nothing is silently changed without a trace.

### What students and parents can see

Only **published** results — never drafts, never partial subjects. A Student sees their own results; a Parent sees each linked child's results, separately, never mixed together.

### Guided setup vs. Advanced Management

**What is this?** Two ways to set up an assessment system — a step-by-step wizard for most schools, and a more detailed editor for anyone who needs finer control.

**Guided setup ("Create Assessment System")** is the recommended starting point for every school — it asks one plain question at a time and never uses technical terms.

**Advanced Management** (found tucked under a collapsible section on the same page) is the same underlying system, just presented as a full editor — useful for going back later to rename an individual assessment, adjust a grading scale, or remove an old assignment. Nothing is created in Advanced Management that the guided wizard couldn't also have created — it's the same system, just a more detailed view of it.

### Worked example: from setup to a published result

**Sunrise Academy, Class 9, Mathematics — First Term:**

1. The School Administrator opens **Create Assessment System** and names it "Class 9 Standard."
2. Chooses **"Yes, we use terms"** and adds "First Term" and "Second Term."
3. Under First Term, adds two assessments: **Classwork** (Full Marks 20) and **Term Exam** (Full Marks 80).
4. Chooses **"Marks with a grade"**, and picks the school's existing "National Scale" grading scale.
5. Chooses **"Whole grade"** — this marking scheme applies to every subject Class 9 takes.
6. Reviews the summary and confirms — the assessment system is created.
7. Later, the Mathematics teacher opens **Enter Marks** for Class 9 Mathematics, First Term, and records each student's Classwork and Term Exam marks. A student who was absent for the exam is marked **Absent** rather than given a zero score that looks like a real result.
8. Once every student has both marks recorded, the teacher opens **Review Results** — everyone shows as complete.
9. The teacher clicks **Publish**. From this moment, every Class 9 student and their parent can see their Mathematics, First Term result — with the grade the National Scale assigns to their percentage.
10. A week later, the teacher notices one student's Classwork mark was entered wrong. They correct it directly — the result stays published, the new mark is now what the family sees, and a record shows exactly what was corrected and when.

## 7. Report Cards

### Viewing results

**What is this?** A single page pulling together everything published about a student — every subject's result, attendance, and shared evaluations — in one formatted view.

**Who can use it?** The student themselves, their parent, or school staff.

**How to use it:** Open the Report Card from the student's dashboard (or, for staff, from the Student Profile page).

### Subject results

Each subject shows its total marks, percentage, and (if the school's grading scale supports one) a letter grade — including a breakdown by term, if the school uses terms.

### Grades

A letter grade (like "A" or "B+") is only shown for a subject whose assessment system uses a grading scale. A marks-only subject simply shows the percentage, with no grade attached — nothing is invented to fill the gap.

### GPA

**What is this?** A single overall number summarizing a student's grades across every subject that has one.

**Important rule:** MEGA.EDU's GPA is explicitly **unweighted** — every subject counts equally, regardless of how many periods/hours it takes up in the timetable. It's always clearly labeled "Unweighted GPA" so nobody mistakes it for a credit-weighted number. If a school's grading scale doesn't produce a grade point at all (some schools use label-only grades with no numeric point), the Report Card instead uses an average percentage across published subjects as the comparable figure.

### Published vs. unpublished results

A Report Card only ever shows a subject once it's been published — an unpublished subject simply doesn't appear on the student's or parent's view of the Report Card at all. Staff viewing the same student's profile can see every subject, including unpublished ones, clearly labeled with their publishing status, so a teacher or admin always knows what a family can and cannot currently see.

## 8. Promotion

### Promotion decisions

**What is this?** The end-of-year decision about what happens to each student — promoted to the next grade, repeating the current grade, transferred to another school, or leaving the school altogether.

**Who can use it?** School Administrator only.

**How to use it:** From a grade's Class Overview, select the students you want to decide on, and choose one action to apply to all of them at once. If you want different students to have different outcomes, apply the decisions in separate batches (e.g. select the promoted group first, apply Promote; then select the repeaters, apply Repeat).

### Repeat

Selecting Repeat keeps a student in the same grade for the next school year. MEGA.EDU defaults the target grade to the student's *current* grade for this action.

### Transfer

Selecting Transfer records that a student has left for another school. No target grade is needed.

### Leave

Selecting Leave records that a student has left the school altogether. No target grade is needed.

### How student history is maintained

Every promotion decision — for every student, every time — is permanently recorded: what the decision was, who made it, and when. Nothing about a student's past is ever overwritten; a repeated year and the year the student eventually completed that grade both stay in the record as two separate entries. A student's section is **never** automatically carried into a new school year for any of these decisions — it's always assigned fresh.

**A student is never silently skipped.** If a school year ends and a student was never given a decision, they don't just disappear — they show up in a clearly visible **Pending** list until an administrator either records the missing decision or manually places them in the new year.

## 9. Dashboards

### School Administrator

Your dashboard is the control center for everything above — school profile, staff and student approval, sessions, grades and sections, subjects, teacher assignments, attendance, evaluations, meetings, assessments, and promotion, all in one place, organized by tab.

### Teacher

Your dashboard shows the grades, sections, and subjects you're assigned to, with direct links into whichever of those you're responsible for that day — taking attendance, recording teaching progress, running a unit test, writing an evaluation, or entering assessment marks. If you're a Class or Section Teacher, you'll also see that responsibility called out separately.

### Student

Your dashboard shows your own school status, your interests and credited skills, any courses you're enrolled in, and — once you're placed into a grade — your Teaching Progress, Test Results, Recent Attendance, any evaluations shared with you, and your published Assessment Results and Report Card.

### Parent

Your dashboard shows, for each of your linked children, the same progress information they see themselves — school status, teaching progress, test results, recent attendance, shared evaluations, published assessment results — plus your Parent-Teacher Meetings, which your child never sees on their own dashboard.

### My Profile

Every role — School Administrator, Teacher, Student, and Parent — has a **My Profile** page, reachable from the small avatar next to your name in the top menu. It's a simple, focused page: your profile photo, your name, your email, your MEGA ID, your school (where applicable), and your role(s).

**Your profile photo belongs to your MEGA.EDU identity, and can appear wherever you participate in the network** — your dashboard, class rosters, attendance lists, and anywhere else your identity is shown — the same photo everywhere, because it's one photo attached to your one MEGA ID, not something you set up separately for each role.

**Uploading a photo:** From My Profile, choose **Upload photo** and pick a PNG, JPEG, or WebP image up to 2MB.

**Changing your photo:** Choosing a new photo replaces the old one — there's no separate "replace" step, just upload again.

**Removing your photo:** Choose **Remove** to clear it. There's nothing wrong with not having a photo — see below.

**No photo yet?** You're not left with a blank space — MEGA.EDU shows a neat initials badge instead (for example, "DA" for Demo Admin), colored consistently just for you, so you're always recognizable even before you've uploaded anything.

MEGA.EDU includes a realistic, fully fictional demonstration environment — a ready-made example of the platform in use, so you can explore how schools, teachers, students, parents, classes, assessments, and results all fit together without needing to set any of it up yourself first.

## 11. Certificates and MEGA Academy

### What's actually available today

- **Free online courses** — organizations can publish courses on MEGA Academy; any teacher or student can enroll in a free one and work through its modules and lessons.
- **Course completion certificates** — completing a free course automatically issues a certificate the moment the enrollment is marked complete. You can view it on a dedicated preview page, or share the public verification link with anyone (no login required for them to check it's real).

### Not available yet (Coming later)

- **Paid/premium courses** — a course with a price attached cannot currently be enrolled in; only free courses work today.
- **Grade-completion certificates** — a certificate issued for finishing a grade (as opposed to a course) is designed for, but not built yet.
- **Downloadable PDF certificates and QR-code verification** — today's certificate is a styled on-screen page with a verification code you can check manually; a downloadable PDF and a scannable QR code are both planned but not available yet.

## 12. Frequently Asked Questions

**Q: Can a student see their marks before they're published?**
No. A mark that's been entered but not yet published is completely invisible to the student and their parent — only staff can see draft marks.

**Q: If a teacher corrects a mark after publishing, does the family get re-notified or does the result go back to "unpublished"?**
The result stays published and the correction is visible immediately — it never reverts to draft. A record of the correction (before and after) is always kept.

**Q: Why does a student who's already been decided "repeating" still show up in the class list?**
Because the school year isn't over yet — they're still physically in the class. The Class Overview shows everyone currently in the grade, whether or not next year's decision has already been recorded.

**Q: Can a student in one section rank higher than a student in another section in the Top 5?**
Yes. The Top 5 ranking is grade-wide, not section-wise — it's calculated once across every section together, not separately per section. A student from any section can appear anywhere in the Top 5.

**Q: What happens if my school doesn't use sections at all?**
Nothing changes — sections are entirely optional. Every student without a section simply appears together in an "Unassigned / No Section" group.

**Q: Can a parent request a Parent-Teacher Meeting?**
Not yet. Today, meetings are always scheduled by the school or a teacher; a parent can only view meetings that have already been scheduled.

**Q: Does my child see Parent-Teacher Meetings on their own dashboard?**
No — meetings are visible only to parents and staff. This is a deliberate, structural rule, not a setting that can be turned on.

**Q: Can I delete a section or subject once it's been used?**
No — sections and subjects can be deactivated (hidden from future use) but never permanently deleted, so any student's history that references them always stays meaningful.

**Q: What's the difference between a Unit Test and the Assessment system?**
A Unit Test is a quick, informal check tied to one teaching chapter. The Assessment system is the school's official, report-card-driving marking scheme. They're kept completely separate — a Unit Test score is never automatically pulled into an official assessment result.

**Q: Can I buy a course on MEGA Academy?**
Not yet — only free courses can currently be enrolled in.

**Q: Is "Roll No." on the Class Overview a permanent student ID?**
No — it's simply each student's position in the list within their section, recalculated every time the page is viewed. It isn't stored anywhere as a permanent student record.

## 13. Glossary of Terms

| Term | Meaning |
|---|---|
| **MEGA ID** | A person's single account on MEGA.EDU, usable across every role they hold |
| **Profile Photo / Avatar** | A person's picture, attached to their MEGA ID and shown wherever their identity appears; shows as an initials badge when none is uploaded |
